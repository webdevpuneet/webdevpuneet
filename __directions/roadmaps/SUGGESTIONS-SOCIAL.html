How to use this Social post templates Change log

How to use this

Each idea has a status: Suggested (not started), Building (in progress) or Done (shipped, kept here as a record). Newest first inside each group.

Rule for what gets added: an idea goes here only if it fills a real gap (something visitors look for that the site does not have, or a workaround a current template forces). Ideas for other areas live in their own files; start from the Suggestions index.

Recommended order, and organising a growing library

Suggested

Added 2026-11-03 · 45 templates now, heading past 60–70 with the occasions and batch 2, so finding the right one starts to matter as much as adding more

Build order:

  1. Now (dates are close): Cyber Monday, Giving Tuesday, Advent / Day N tile, Holiday hours, Year in review.
  2. Reusable worldwide: National / Independence Day (flag colours) and Festival greeting (motif + palette), then Awareness Day and Industry Day.
  3. Evergreen gaps, batch 2: 5-star review, flash sale countdown, free resource, open for bookings, new video, new podcast episode, then new arrival, referral, weekly roundup, anniversary.
  4. Spring 2027: Lunar New Year, Ramadan / Eid, Women's Day, Easter, Mother's Day.

Why this order (most return and engagement for the effort): seasonal pages bring the biggest traffic spikes, but only if they are live and indexed about 3 weeks before the date; evergreen templates are what people come back for every week; the organising features below turn one visit into several (more pages per visit, return visits, longer editor sessions). Check Clarity and Statcounter monthly for which templates are opened and exported, and build more of what wins.

Organising the library as it grows:

  • Filter by goal and by industry on the gallery (sell, announce, educate, engage, celebrate; retail, food, real estate, SaaS, coaching…). Tags on each template, chips on the gallery, crawlable pages like the UI snippet tag pages.
  • "Coming up" occasions strip at the top of /social-post-templates/: the next 4–6 occasions with the date, days left and the matching template. A reason to come back every month, and internal links to seasonal pages before their search peak.
  • Split Holidays & Seasonal once it passes about 15: Shopping days, Festivals & faith, National days, Awareness & industry days.
  • Search and favourites in the gallery (the UI snippet bookmark pattern can be reused).
  • Post series: one design, many posts (Advent day 1–24, "tip 1 of 10", carousel slides) with the number filled in automatically and one ZIP export.
  • Collections / packs: "Black Friday pack" (teaser, countdown, sale, last chance, thank you), "Launch pack", "Restaurant month": a ready set per campaign, paired with the matching emails.

Design and animation bar for every new template (decided 2026-11-02):

  • Creative, great-looking design: its own layout and a clear idea, not a recolour of an existing template. Check it at every post size (square, portrait, Story with its safe areas) and with long and short text.
  • Built to animate on export: the video export (MP4 / WebM, in render.js) animates every [data-edit] text slot and [data-image] photo in reading order (rise, fade or pop). Anything meant to animate must be one of those slots, and the slots must be ordered so the reveal reads well (headline, then detail, then the call to action). Decorative shapes are not animated today.
  • Check the video: export the animated video once per new template and watch it before calling the template done.

Idea to make exports feel richer: let a template mark decorative parts as animatable (a data-anim hook) so shapes, badges, ratings and bars move too: stars filling, a progress bar growing, numbers counting up, a countdown ticking, underlines drawing. Countdown, 5-star review, big stat, flash sale and pricing card would benefit first.

Rule for every new social post template: animate each part in a way that fits its theme (decided 2026-11-02). Not a generic fade on everything: each moving part should do what that thing would do in real life. A flame flickers, a heart beats, a tick gets stamped, a number counts up.

  • How to build it: mark the element with data-anim="<motion>" (optionally data-at, data-dur, data-origin); the motions live in render.js. Reveals, which play once in reading order: pop / tick, stamp, draw-x, draw-y, wipe-r, wipe-u, slide-l/r/t/b, settle, count, countdown, type. Loops: pulse, breathe, wobble, sway, bob, nudge-x/y, twinkle, blink, spin, fly, burst, fall, scroll-bg, scroll-y (a floor rolling towards you), wave (a flag ripple by position), flicker, float, drift. Add a new motion only when none fits. Motions use the individual CSS properties (translate, rotate, scale), so they do not fight a template's own transform or its text and photo reveal.
  • Status: Done 2026-11-02 all 45 templates have theme motion (list below). Checked in the editor with Play on every template: each one moves, keeps moving as intended and returns to its normal state.
  • Check each one: press Play in the editor, watch the whole loop at phone size, and confirm that nothing is clipped, nothing leaves its place for good and the post settles back to normal.

Dummy placeholder images instead of plain gradients (added 2026-11-02, from the New Blog Article cover):

  • Problem: an empty photo slot shows a plain colour gradient, so a template looks unfinished in the gallery, in previews and in exports until the user adds a photo.
  • Idea: small illustrated SVG placeholders, drawn in white over the template's accent gradient so they keep its colours: a landscape (sun, hills, clouds), a portrait (head and shoulders), a product screenshot (a window with a sidebar and bars) and an article cover (chart and text bars). A template names the one it wants with data-ph on the slot; the default is the landscape.
  • Rules: self-contained (data URI, no external files), never exported as if it were the user's photo, replaced the moment a photo is added, and optional slots stay hidden when empty.
  • Later: a few more (food, building, team group, logo wall) and a "use sample photo" button that fills slots with free-to-use stock images for demos.

WhatsApp sharing (added 2026-11-02):

  • Works today: download the PNG or JPG and send it in a chat, a group or a Channel, or post the Story size (1080×1920) as a WhatsApp Status. WhatsApp compresses photos, so for crisp text send the file as a document.
  • Share button (idea): a "Share" item in the Export menu that opens the phone's share sheet with the image attached (the Web Share API with files), so WhatsApp is two taps away on a phone without saving to the gallery first. Falls back to a download on desktop.
  • Video for Status: WhatsApp plays MP4 but not WebM, and Status videos are limited to about 30 seconds. The animated export is MP4 in Chrome (6 seconds by default), so it fits; show a note when the browser can only make WebM.
  • Size note: a "WhatsApp Status" label on the Story size in the size picker, since many users in India look for it by that name.

For each template: curated, polished, non-generic SEO text (written for that one template, never a pattern with the name swapped; see 06-QUALITY-BARS.txt) within the length limits, preview image, category page entry, sitemap and llms.txt.
