How to use this

Each idea has a status: Suggested (not started), Building (in progress) or Done (shipped, kept here as a record). When an idea is built, change its status and add a line to the change log at the bottom.

Rule for what gets added: an idea goes here only if it fills a real gap (something visitors look for that the site doesn't have, or a workaround a current template uses), not just because it would be nice.

Section templates (Website Section Templates library)

The library has 78 sections in 23 categories (checked 2026-10-31). The gaps below come from comparing it with what the 24 website templates actually need.

1. Opening Hours & Location

Done

Category: Contact · Added 2026-10-26

Why: local businesses are the largest group of website templates, and "are they open now, and where?" is the first thing their visitors look for. Contact With Map shows the address but not the hours. It also helps local SEO through openingHoursSpecification data.

Templates it would improve: restaurant, dental, salon, fitness, local service, hotel, law firm, church, school (six of these already use Contact With Map).

What it contains:

  • Weekly hours table with today's row highlighted; each row has day, open, close, a "Closed" option and split shifts (9–1 and 3–7).
  • Live "Open now · closes 9 pm" badge worked out in the browser from the hours (time zone is a setting).
  • Holiday / special-hours notice line ("Closed 25 Dec").
  • Address, phone, email, "Get directions" button and a map or image panel; a booking or call button.
  • Two layouts: hours beside the map, and a compact strip for footers or narrow columns.
  • Standard editing: text and hours from forms, own images and fonts, export HTML, React or Vue.

Also add: LocalBusiness opening-hours markup to the exported page; place it in the restaurant, dental, salon, local-service and church templates.

2. Event Schedule & Countdown

Suggested

Category: Features or Stats & logos · Added 2026-10-26

Why: a countdown plus a day-by-day agenda is the core of event, wedding and launch pages. Today the event template uses Alternating Features for the agenda and the wedding template reuses Property Listings.

Templates it would improve: event, wedding, startup launch & waitlist; also online-course cohorts and nonprofit campaigns.

  • Countdown to a date and time (days, hours, minutes, seconds) with a "started" message when it ends.
  • Agenda with day tabs, time, title, speaker and room; optional "Add to calendar" link.

8. Scroll-Driven 3D Carousel (GSAP)

DoneTop pick

Category: Scroll & motion · Added 2026-10-27 · Built as the scroll-ring section (3D Scroll Carousel, v1.2.0): checked in a browser 2026-10-31 (5 cards, the view changes as the page scrolls, no errors) and its preview image exists. The variants and extras below are what the idea started from; further ideas for it (drag to rotate, idle auto-rotate, a changing caption) are still open.

Why: a popular "showcase" look (centre card faces forward, side cards curve away in perspective, glowing rounded cards) that the library does not have. It suits product, portfolio and app-screens sections, and reuses the existing GSAP + ScrollTrigger runtime.

  • Layout: the section is pinned; cards sit on a ring (perspective, transform-style: preserve-3d, rotateY() + translateZ() per card). Scrolling rotates the ring (ScrollTrigger pin + scrub), so the active card changes as the page scrolls.
  • Depth: side cards get lower opacity, slight blur and scale from their angle; the active card gets the glow border.
  • Variants: Cylinder ring (cards curve away on both sides, as in the reference), Coverflow (flat row, angled sides), Tilted stack.
  • Editable: 5-8 cards (image, title, link), accent/glow colour, ring radius, scroll length, glow on/off.
  • Behaviour: dots and arrows as well as scroll; click a side card to bring it forward; swipe on touch.
  • Fallbacks: phones and prefers-reduced-motion become a plain swipeable strip; content visible without JavaScript.
  • Build: one new scroll behaviour in the existing scroll-section setup, no new plugin needed (core + ScrollTrigger; optional Observer for swipe).

12. Ecommerce kit: shop, cart, add to cart, checkout

SuggestedTop pick

Category: new sections + a website template · Added 2026-10-31 · Today only product-grid (a button that links out), the listings sections and the Product Landing Page Store template exist, so nobody can build a real shop with a cart.

The constraint: exports are plain HTML with no server, so the kit cannot take payments or keep stock by itself. The cart can live in the browser (a few lines of JavaScript and localStorage); the payment must go to a hosted checkout. The site's card details must never touch our HTML. This also suits the site's angle: people see exactly how a cart works.

How checkout would work (pick the options to support)

OptionWhat it is
Payment link per product (Stripe, PayPal, Razorpay)Each product has a Pay link; the cart sends the buyer to the hosted page. No code, works for single items and small shops.
Order by messageThe checkout form sends the cart as an email, WhatsApp message or form service (Formspree and similar). Good for local shops, bakeries, services, India and other markets without card checkout.
Hosted shop widget (Snipcart, Shopify Buy Button, Ecwid)The section outputs the product data attributes the widget needs and loads its script. Real cart, stock, tax and orders without writing a backend.
Digital downloads (Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy)Product buttons open their overlay checkout. Fits templates, ebooks and courses.

Sections to build

  • 1. Product grid with working Add to cart (upgrade product-grid): quantity, a cart counter in the navbar, filters and search.
  • 2. Product detail: the product page itself, the most important page of a shop. What it should have is listed in full under "The product page" below.
  • 3. Cart drawer / mini cart: slides in from the right, line items, quantity steppers, subtotal, Checkout button.
  • 4. Cart page: full table of items, promo code box (display only unless the shop widget handles it), shipping estimate, order summary.
  • 5. Checkout page: contact and shipping form, order summary, payment choices from the table above, trust badges and policy links. No card fields in our markup.
  • 6. Order confirmation / thank you: order summary, what happens next, a continue-shopping link.
  • 7. Extras: wishlist (heart button), free-shipping progress bar, trust badges row (secure payment, returns, delivery), recently viewed, collection banner, size guide modal.

Website template

An Online Store multi-page template: Home, Shop (all products), Product, Cart, Checkout, Order confirmed, About, Contact, plus Privacy, Shipping & returns and Terms (a shop needs these pages). Variants by industry: fashion, electronics, food and bakery, handmade, digital products.

Build order

  1. A small shared cart script (add, remove, quantity, total, localStorage, a cart counter) used by every shop section, written so people can read it.
  2. Product grid upgrade, product detail, cart drawer.
  3. Cart page, checkout page with the "order by message" and "payment link" options first, confirmation page.
  4. The Online Store website template, then the hosted-widget option.

Honest limits to tell visitors on the page: no stock control, no tax or shipping rules and no order management in the plain version. For a bigger shop the template should point people to a hosted shop (Shopify, WooCommerce) or use the hosted-widget option.

How to build them fast

  • A template is mostly content. It is a list of sections, a theme preset, a font pair and real text and pictures for that industry. A new template reuses the same sections, so the work is the writing, the images and the page list.
  • Work in batches of about ten in one industry group at a time (health, then food, then trades), so the images, wording and SEO text can be done together.
  • Industry versions of one base: Local Service Business becomes plumber, electrician, cleaner and so on by changing the text, photos, colours and a few sections. The same goes for the clinic family (doctor, vet, physio, optician).
  • Each template needs: one-page and multi-page versions, an SEO title and description within the limits in 06-QUALITY-BARS.txt, images with alt text, a preview image, a demo export and upload, and an entry in the industry filters. The steps are in __directions/ideas/ADDING-SECTIONS-TO-WEBSITE-TEMPLATES.txt and the website templates update checklist.
  • Pick the order by search demand, not by guess: check Search Console and a keyword tool for "[industry] website template" and build the biggest first.
  • Sections these need that do not exist yet (see idea 11): Opening Hours & Location (almost every local business), About / Our story, Reviews summary, Event schedule, and the shop sections of idea 12 for all the shops.

Industry needs will change the sections too

Plan for this: each industry demands sections the library does not have, and some of the 78 sections will need an industry version or a new option. So building a website template is two jobs: first a section gap check (what does this industry's website need, what exists, what is missing or needs changing), then the template itself. Write the findings into this document under the industry before building.

Rule of thumb: if an existing section only needs different fields, wording or one extra option, add an option or variant to it (for example a monthly / yearly switch on pricing) rather than copying it into a new section. Create a new section only when the layout or behaviour is genuinely different.

A running list of trendy things worth trying, added as ideas come up while building. Newest first. Each one says where it would go.

14. Trendy features for templates and sections

Suggested

Added 2026-10-31 · Cross-cutting ideas, not tied to one section.

  • WhatsApp click-to-chat button: a floating button for local businesses, shops and clinics. Very common and useful worldwide; fits next to Back to top and the social share bar.
  • Booking calendar embed (Cal.com, Calendly): a section that loads the embed with the person's link. Needed by clinics, coaches, salons and consultants.
  • Scroll-driven storytelling: pinned images that change as you read, and zoom-ins on details (we have the scroll sections), for product pages, portfolios and brand pages.
  • Mesh and animated gradient backgrounds: a gradient that slowly moves, as a new artwork and as a hero background (the gradient scheme and artwork already exist).
  • Bento grids: mixed-size tiles for features and portfolios (Features Bento exists; extend it to portfolios and product highlights).
  • Dark / light mode toggle on the exported site, using the theme presets we already have as the two modes.
  • Page transitions between pages of a multi-page export (the browser's view transitions, a few lines of CSS), so the site feels like an app.
  • Story-style testimonials: short customer videos in a tap-through row, like social stories.
  • Scroll progress bar and reading-time label for blog posts.
  • Language switcher and right-to-left support: two-language sites (English plus Hindi, Arabic and so on) from one template.
  • Lottie / animated illustration section: a place to drop an animation file.
  • Micro-interactions: magnetic buttons, tilt cards, animated counters and an animated checkmark on form success (some already exist; make them options in the Look panel).
  • Mobile first details: bottom sheets, sticky call and book buttons, and a bottom tab bar for app-like sites.
  • AI-ready touches: a clean llms.txt (already exported), plus an "Ask about this page" chat placeholder people can connect to their own assistant.
  • Performance badge: show the exported page's size and a check list (images sized, fonts limited), a nice learning angle.

Keep adding to this list whenever a good idea comes up, and move an idea into its own numbered entry once it is planned.
